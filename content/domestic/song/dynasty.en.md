# Song Dynasty Overview: Northern and Southern Song (960–1279)
The Song dynasty (960–1279) divides into Northern Song (960–1127) and Southern Song (1127–1279). Zhao Kuangyin 赵匡胤 (circa 927–976, disputed) took the throne at Chenqiao, made Kaifeng the capital, and with his successor progressively absorbed Jingnan, Later Shu, Southern Han, Southern Tang, Wuyue, and Northern Han by 979. Centralizing policy removed military governors' power and used fiscal intendants and prefectural supervisors, creating a strong-center pattern.
Fan Zhongyan's 范仲淹 Qingli reforms soon ended; Wang Anshi 王安石 (1021–1086) carried out New Policies under Emperor Shenzong, opposed by Sima Guang 司马光 (1019–1086) and the old party, and factional strife continued. Jin armies advanced south in 1125–1127, the Jingkang 靖康 disaster carried Emperors Huizong and Qinzong north, and Kaifeng fell, ending Northern Song.
Zhao Gou 赵构 (1107–1187) restored Song rule in the south with Lin'an as capital. Generals including Yue Fei 岳飞 resisted the Jin until the Shaoxing peace of 1141 fixed the Huai–Dasanguan frontier with Song tribute payments. Failed northern campaigns under Xiaozong and Han Tuozhou 韩侂胄 followed; after the joint destruction of Jin in 1234, Song–Mongol conflict resumed, ending with the fall of Lin'an and the deaths at Yashan 崖山 in 1279. Captive counts and abdication details are disputed.
