# Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms Overview (907–979)
After Zhu Wen 朱温 (circa 852–912, disputed) replaced Tang in 907, the Central Plain saw five short successive dynasties: Later Liang (907–923), Later Tang under Li Cunxu 李存勖 (circa 885–926, disputed) (923–936), Later Jin under Shi Jingtang 石敬瑭 (circa 892–942, disputed) (936–947), Later Han (947–951), and Later Zhou under Guo Wei 郭威 (circa 904–954, disputed) and Chai Rong 柴荣 (circa 921–959, disputed) (951–960). These eight surnames and fourteen rulers in five decades continued the late-Tang military-governor order.
In the south and Sichuan, ten concurrent kingdoms including Wu, Southern Tang, Wuyue, Min, Chu, Jingnan, Former and Later Shu, Southern Han, Southern Ping, and Northern Han rose and fell at different dates; the roster itself varies among Song accounts and is treated as disputed. Shi Jingtang's cession of the Yan-Yun region, the Later Zhou northern campaigns of 954–959, and the Chenqiao 陈桥 mutiny of 960, in which the Song replaced Zhou, frame reunification, completed with the capture of Northern Han in 979.
Military governors, the growing Bureau of Military Affairs and Three Fiscal Agencies, and the career of Feng Dao 冯道 across five courts illustrate institutional continuity.
