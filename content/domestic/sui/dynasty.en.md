# Sui Dynasty Overview (581–618)
The Sui dynasty (581–618) reunified north and south after centuries of division. Yang Jian 杨坚 (circa 541–604, disputed), Emperor Wen, replaced Northern Zhou in 581 and destroyed Chen in 589, completing reunification. His Kaihuang 开皇 administration lightened taxes, maintained the equal-field and militia systems, compiled the Kaihuang Code, and developed the Three Departments and Six Ministries in embryo.
Emperor Yang 杨广 (circa 569–618, disputed) moved to Luoyang, opened the Grand Canal by linking channels including the Tongji Canal from 605, and projected power toward Liaodong. Three campaigns against Goguryeo in 612–614 exhausted the state; the Yang Xuangan 杨玄感 revolt of 613, the Yanmen encirclement of 615, and the Li Yuan 李渊 rising of 617 followed. Yang Guang was killed at Jiangdu in 618, and the puppet child emperor Yang You 杨侑 (circa 605–619, disputed) yielded to the Tang.
The founding year of the presented-scholar examination, judgments on Emperor Yang shaped by early Tang historians, and responsibility for the Chen campaign remain disputed. Territorial extent is schematic and the Liaodong and Hexi limits are uncertain.
The equal-field, rented-labor modulation, fubing militia, Kaihuang legal code, and courier relay network are treated in the official entries, and the Sui–Tang transition is cross-referenced under Tang. Sources pending page verification, no internet verification.
