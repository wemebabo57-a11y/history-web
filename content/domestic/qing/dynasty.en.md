# Qing Dynasty Overview (1616–1912)
The Qing dynasty grew from the Jurchen Later Jin. Nurhaci 努尔哈赤 (circa 1559–1626) unified the Jurchens, founded the state in 1616, and built the Eight Banners; Huangtaiji 皇太极 (circa 1592–1643) changed the name to Qing in 1636, and Qing forces entered the pass in 1644, making Beijing the capital with Shengjing retained as a secondary seat. Central government used the Grand Secretariat, Six Boards, and after Yongzheng the Grand Council, with governors-general and governors in the provinces.
The Kangxi 玄烨 (1654–1722), Yongzheng 胤禛 (circa 1678–1735), and Qianlong 弘历 (1711–1799) reigns suppressed the Three Feudatories, incorporated Taiwan, expanded frontiers, regularized the poll-tax merger and permanent tax quotas, and enforced maritime restrictions. After the Opium Wars, unequal treaties, the Taiping rebellion, the Self-Strengthening movement, defeat by Japan in 1895, the Hundred Days reform, the Boxer rising, and late constitutional moves reshaped the state; Empress Dowager Cixi 慈禧 (circa 1835–1908) held effective power from 1861 to 1908.
The 1911 Revolution ended imperial rule in 1912. Judgments on the High Qing, the reasons for the closed-door policy, the nature of Taiping and Boxer movements, and the chief cause of the fall present multiple views and are left disputed.
