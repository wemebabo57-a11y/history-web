# Ming Dynasty Overview (1368–1644)
The Ming dynasty (1368–1644) was founded by Zhu Yuanzhang 朱元璋 (1328–1398), who proclaimed the dynasty in 1368 after the Red Turban wars. Early Ming abolished the Central Secretariat, concentrated authority in the emperor, staffed administration through the Six Ministries and later the Grand Secretariat, and paired garrison guards with lijia community organization. Sixteen emperors are counted in the transmitted line, excluding the later Southern Ming courts.
Zhu Di 朱棣 (1360–1424), the Yongle emperor, moved the capital to Beijing, retained a Nanjing establishment, and sent Zheng He 郑和 on maritime voyages. After the Tumu crisis of 1449, civil officials gained weight against the throne; Yu Qian 于谦 (1398–1457) organized the defense of Beijing. The Single Whip fiscal reform, Wanli aid to Korea, the Sarhu defeat, and factional strife marked the middle and late periods, with Zhang Juzheng 张居正 (circa 1525–1582) and Wei Zhongxian 魏忠贤 (circa 1568–1627) as prominent figures.
The Chongzhen emperor Zhu Youjian 朱由检 (1611–1644) faced fiscal collapse, rebellion, and Manchu pressure until Li Zicheng 李自成 entered Beijing in 1644. The Jianwen emperor's fate, responsibility for Tumu, the degree of Wanli negligence, and the chief cause of the fall remain disputed.
