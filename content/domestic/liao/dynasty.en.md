# Liao Dynasty Overview (916–1125)
The Liao dynasty (916–1125) was founded by the Khitan leader Yelü Abaoji 耶律阿保机 (circa 872–926, disputed), who united the Khitan tribes, proclaimed himself emperor in 916, and fixed the supreme capital at Linhuang. His successor Yelü Deguang 耶律德光 destroyed Later Jin, gained the Sixteen Prefectures of Yan and Yun, and in 947 adopted the dynastic name Liao, securing agrarian households and revenue along the farming–pastoral frontier.
After Emperor Jingzong, Empress Dowager Xiao Xiao Chuo 萧绰 (circa 953–1009, disputed) acted as regent for about twenty-seven years, employing Yelü Xiezhen 耶律斜轸 and Han Derang 韩德让, regularizing administration and household registers. Repeated Song–Liao wars ended with the Chanyuan 澶渊 covenant of 1005, making the two courts nominal brothers, with annual Song payments of silver and silk and frontier markets sustaining roughly a century of peace.
From Emperor Daozong the administration decayed; the last ruler Yelü Yanxi 耶律延禧 lost control as the Jurchen rose, and the Song–Jin Maritime Alliance attacked Liao from two sides until Jin captured the emperor in 1125. Yelü Dashi 耶律大石 later moved west to found Western Liao. Territorial limits are schematic, with Yan–Yun held in the south.
