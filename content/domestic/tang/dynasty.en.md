# Tang Dynasty Overview (618–907)
The Tang dynasty (618–907) lasted nearly three centuries. Li Yuan 李渊 (circa 566–635, disputed) founded the dynasty in 618; his son Li Shimin 李世民 (circa 598–649, disputed) seized power in the Xuanwu Gate 玄武门 incident of 626 and presided over the Zhenguan 贞观 administration. After Emperor Gaozong, Wu Zetian 武则天 (circa 624–705, disputed) ruled from behind and then openly, founding the Zhou in 690 before the Shenlong restoration of 705 returned the Tang.
Emperor Xuanzong 李隆基 (circa 685–762, disputed) brought the Kaiyuan 开元 high point, followed by the An–Shi Rebellion 安史之乱 (755–763) led by An Lushan 安禄山 (circa 703–757, disputed), after which provincial commands, eunuchs, and fiscal strain reshaped politics. The Two-Tax reform of 780 steadied revenue; the Huang Chao 黄巢 uprising (875–884) broke the dynasty's foundations, and Zhu Wen 朱温 replaced Tang in 907.
Institutions evolved from equal-field and fubing service toward recruitment and the Two-Tax system, with Chang'an as capital and Luoyang as eastern capital. Judgments on Xuanwu Gate, on Wu Zhou, and on Yang Guifei's 杨贵妃 fate at Mawei remain disputed.
