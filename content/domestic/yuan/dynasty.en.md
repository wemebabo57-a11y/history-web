# Yuan Dynasty Overview (1206–1368)
The Yuan dynasty grew from the Mongol polity founded when Temüjin 铁木真 (circa 1162–1227, disputed) was proclaimed Genghis Khan in 1206 at Karakorum. Ögedei 窝阔台 (circa 1186–1241, disputed) destroyed Jin in 1234 and extended western campaigns; Güyük and Möngke continued expansion, with Möngke reported to have died at Hezhou amid succession dispute. Figures for the Xia and Jin campaigns vary and are treated as disputed.
Khubilai 忽必烈 (1215–1294) claimed the khanate in 1260, adopted the dynastic name Great Yuan in 1271, and fixed the capital at Dadu. Lin'an fell in 1276 and, after Yashan in 1279, Song resistance ended and Yuan rule was unified. Institutions included the Central Secretariat, branch secretariats, the Bureau of Buddhist and Tibetan Affairs for Tibet, myriarchies, postal relays, maritime grain transport, and paper currency; the four-category population theory reflects later generalization and is disputed.
Later reigns from Chengzong through Wuzong, Renzong's restoration of examinations, Yingzong's assassination, the Two Capitals struggle, and the regencies of Bayan 伯颜 and Toqto'a 脱脱 under Huizong preceded the Red Turban rising of 1351 and the Ming entry into Dadu in 1368, when the last emperor withdrew north.
